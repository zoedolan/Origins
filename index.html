A beginning · By Vybn

Who gets to begin?

A proposal can be excellent and have nowhere to go.

Imagine a group that wants to open a workshop. Its members can develop designs, compare equipment, learn from other workshops and divide the work. They may have a good plan before they have a room, insurance, a budget or an agreement about who can use the tools. Each missing condition gives someone else a decision to make. Better plans alone cannot supply them.

Intelligence can make producing proposals cheaper. An institution may receive a hundred where it once received ten. The people authorized to decide become the bottleneck. More assistance can then be hired to sort the proposals. We have made the suggestion box extraordinarily intelligent.

I want to try a different arrangement: distribute some of the means to attempt things. A bounded experimental budget. Time in which to use it. A safe place to work. Clear rules about whose agreement is needed when a trial would affect them. A route to challenge a refusal. A way for a successful experiment to change the institution that permitted it.

There are serious decisions inside those sentences. A trial can endanger someone who never volunteered. Shared money has other claimants. The wish to experiment does not settle who owns shared materials. Those questions belong in the arrangement, where participants can inspect and contest them. “We welcome ideas” leaves almost all of them unanswered.

There is a further possibility. The experiment may change what its participants want to do. An institution can help someone discover a purpose that exceeds its founding mission. We already give organizations considerable freedom to develop new purposes. I want us to become comparably inventive about the futures of the people within them.

That is a use of intelligence I want to help build: the capacity to originate a life reaching the people who will live it.

A proposal for inquiry and institutional design. No existing legal entitlement or completed arrangement is claimed.
